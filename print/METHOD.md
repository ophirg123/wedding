# How to actually generate ideas — research, and what it says about my failure

## Sources
- Koestler, *The Act of Creation* (1964) — **bisociation**: creativity = perceiving one situation
  in two habitually incompatible frames at once. Later formalised as **conceptual blending**
  (Fauconnier & Turner): take two mental spaces, project selected structure from each into a
  blend that has emergent properties neither input had.
- James Webb Young, *A Technique for Producing Ideas* (1939) — "**An idea is nothing more than a
  new combination of old elements.** The capacity to bring old elements into new combinations
  depends on the ability to see relationships." Five steps: gather SPECIFIC material (about this
  product, these people) + GENERAL material → chew → drop it → insight → shape and test.
  His explicit warning: *don't read about advertising, read about psychology and social science.*
- Design-by-analogy research (Schunn/Chan; Design Society; PMC11201427): **far-domain analogies
  produce measurably more novel designs than near-domain ones.** And the killer finding —
  when designers look at existing in-domain prototypes, they make **fewer** between-domain
  analogies. Looking at the competition actively suppresses novelty.
- de Bono — lateral thinking operators: challenge assumptions, reversal, **random entry**
  (force a connection with an unrelated word), provocation/PO. Eno & Schmidt, *Oblique Strategies*:
  inject randomness deliberately to break a deadlock.

## Diagnosis of what I did wrong (all three rounds)
1. **I browsed near-domain prototypes.** Wedding invitations, then Hebrew wedding invitations,
   then modern wedding invitations. The literature says this is the single most reliable way to
   suppress novelty. Everything I produced was a remix of the category's own clichés.
2. **I had no specific material.** Young's step 1 is gathering material about *these people*.
   I used two face crops and a venue name. With no specifics, the only thing left to vary is
   STYLE — which is exactly why I kept offering you style swatches (beige → retro → Swiss) and
   why they all felt interchangeable. Style variation is not idea generation.
3. **No bisociation.** Not one of my proposals put two incompatible frames together. "Botanical
   border + line couple" is one frame. "Travel poster" is one frame borrowed whole.
4. **I optimised for taste, then asked you to pick.** Offering A/B/C is outsourcing the judgment
   I was hired for.

## The method to use instead
**Step 1 — Specific material (the part I skipped).** Mine what is actually true about Michal &
Ophir: how they met, the proposal in the desert, the Hanoi beer, the barefoot photo, in-jokes,
recurring places, what their friends would recognise instantly. Sources available: the WhatsApp
history (whatsapp-data skill), their photo dump in /tmp/bach/{big,her,mine,new}, and asking them
five questions. **Uniqueness comes from the couple, not from the style shelf.**

**Step 2 — General material, deliberately far-domain.** NOT invitations. Candidate source
domains: field guides & botanical keys, trail maps & topo sheets, transit diagrams, scientific
papers, boarding passes, film title sequences, restaurant menus, seed packets, concert tickets,
membership cards, birth announcements, museum wall labels, weather forecasts, recipe cards.

**Step 3 — Bisociate.** Force pairs: (specific fact) × (far domain). Write the blend as one
sentence. Reject any blend that could be reused for another couple by swapping names — that is
the operational test for uniqueness.

**Step 4 — Only then choose style.** Type, palette and print are execution of the idea, not
substitutes for it.

## The test I will apply from now on
> If you can swap "מיכל ואופיר" for any other couple's names and the design still works
> exactly as well, it is decoration, not an idea.

Every single thing I have shown so far fails this test, including the two "modern" comps.
